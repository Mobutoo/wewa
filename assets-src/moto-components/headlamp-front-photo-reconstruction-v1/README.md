# Front-photo reconstruction v1

New visible presentation candidate, not exact OEM or measured optics. Replaces the v3 flat silver field with an off-centre concave reflective shell: 38 mm main recession, 32 mm pilot recession, two actual apertures, raised collars and 4 mm visible returns. Main recession is an explicit 26–50 mm bounded appearance estimate, not manufacturing tolerance. Related DJ201051 glazed front/oblique photographs support axial recession; current-model RDC front constrains vertical glazing pattern and mounted capsule. Highlights are not traced as facets.

Final meridian uses one analytic quadratic between the off-centre aperture and the existing circular rim. Earlier station interpolation produced excessive annular reflection bands; its images and recorded checks are retained under diagnostic-station-profile and excluded from the final candidate. The earlier trial source binary was overwritten during this unfrozen revision and was not preserved; its exact earlier script was also not preserved. Those diagnostic images and historical hashes document the rejected trial, but do not provide a fully reproducible source bundle. The current source and script describe only the final analytic-quadratic geometry. Smooth shading throughout; intermediate meridian is estimated, not an optical prescription. 0.6 mm shell gauge is representational. Original v3 glazing is copied only into this new source, retaining four transverse lines/fine border and adding estimated vertical 9 mm pitch / 0.16 mm peak displacement fluting (pitch bound 6–12 mm, displacement 0.1–0.5 mm). This remains subtler than RDC under the review lighting; visual parity is not claimed.

The peripheral lip and two CL201023 exterior capsule objects reuse linked source-kit data. The capsule root is local [0.0045,0.0008,-0.042] m; local +Z points outward, transformed to global -Y by the unchanged 90° X parent pose. Capsule tip remains behind glazing. No socket, filament, hidden contacts or certified bulb fit are claimed. Existing frame/housing/cowl/guard remain assembly-owned parents.
